South African mine safety reporting sits under the Mine Health and Safety Act,
which sets out which occurrences must be reported and how quickly. Use the
classification your site's own procedure and appointed manager work to. This
template is a starting point for writing the report; it is not compliance
advice and it does not decide reportability for you.

## Description

What happened, in the order it happened, in plain language. Facts only in this
section: who was where, what was being done, what changed, what was observed.
Keep opinion, blame and conclusions out of it — they belong under root cause.

## Immediate actions taken

1. Made safe: what was isolated, barricaded or stopped, and by whom.
2. Care: first aid or medical treatment given, and where the person was taken.
3. Notified: who was told, at what time, and through what channel.
4. Preserved: the scene, the equipment and the records held for investigation.

## Root cause (preliminary)

Preliminary findings only, pending the full investigation.

- **Immediate cause:** the act or condition directly preceding the incident.
- **Contributing factors:** equipment, procedure, environment, supervision,
  training, fatigue, communication.
- **Underlying cause:** the system that allowed the above to stand.
